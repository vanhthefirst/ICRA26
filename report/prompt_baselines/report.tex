\begin{center}
{\large\textbf{Sketch-Prompted VLA --- three $\pi_{0.5}$ baselines}}\\[2pt]
Aaron \quad\textbar\quad 17 August 2026
\end{center}

\vspace{-6pt}
\textbf{What changed.} Every scene now carries two captions instead of one, so
the dataset is 114 scenes $\times$ 2 prompts $=$ \textbf{228 evaluation rows}.
Same BDDL, same initial states, same sketches, same tiers.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Explicit prompt} --- names the target and the destination by
    category (\emph{``pick up the black bowl and place it on the plate''}). This
    is every caption I have used until now.
  \item \textbf{Ambiguous prompt} --- names neither (\emph{``pick this up and
    place it on that''}). 20 wordings, each keeping its scene's own verb so the
    arms differ in referring expressions only.
  \item \textbf{Tier} --- a property of the \emph{scene}, not the caption:
    \texttt{control} (one-to-one), \texttt{referential} (many-to-one),
    \texttt{directional} (one-to-many), \texttt{both} (many-to-many). Any tier
    can carry either caption.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Headline.} Explicit \textbf{\numExplicitOverall\%} against ambiguous
\textbf{\numAmbiguousOverall\%} sustained success, 1{,}596 trials each. The same
pipeline and checkpoint score 98.2\% on the three standard LIBERO suites.
Control tier: \numExplicitControl\% explicit, \numAmbiguousControl\% ambiguous.

\heading{The two arms, paired}

Pooling the arms and subtracting throws away the pairing. Each scene is run in
both arms at the same 14 rollout indices, and \texttt{stable\_seed} does not read
the prompt type, so trial $k$ of a scene starts from the same state in both arms
--- verified, not assumed: \numPairedSeedMatch\% of the \numPairedTrials{} trial
pairs share an \texttt{init\_state\_hash}. The difference is therefore measured
inside a scene.

\heading{Does the wording carry the result?}

The ambiguous arm draws on 20 wordings so that no single sentence becomes the
finding. Two checks say whether that worked, and one of them does not come back
clean.

Grouping the paired trials by which word the caption uses for the destination
separates wording from scene difficulty, because the explicit rate of each group
is a measure of how hard those scenes are to begin with:

\begin{center}
\captionof{table}{Destination deictic. The explicit column is the control: it is
the same scenes under captions that name everything.}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Destination word & Trials & Explicit (\%) & Ambiguous (\%) & $\Delta$ (pp) \\
\midrule
\texttt{that} / \texttt{that one} & \numDeicticThatN & \numDeicticThatExpl &
  \numDeicticThatAmb & \numDeicticThatDelta \\
\texttt{there} & \numDeicticThereN & \numDeicticThereExpl &
  \numDeicticThereAmb & \numDeicticThereDelta \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two groups' explicit rates agree to within about three points, so the scenes
behind them are of comparable difficulty. Their ambiguous rates do not agree at
all: \numDeicticThereAmb\% against \numDeicticThatAmb\%. Captions ending in
\emph{there} are close to never solved. That is a property of the word, not of
ambiguity, and it depresses the headline --- the \numDeicticThereN{} affected
trials are about a sixth of the arm. I am reporting the pooled number as
measured and flagging this rather than repairing it: changing the bank now would
make the arm incomparable to the one that ran. The right fix is a separate
labelled arm that replaces \emph{there} with \emph{that}, which is a future run,
not an edit to this one.

The second check is the per-template spread, and it is the one that is fine.
Raw per-template success runs from 0\% to 41\%, which looks alarming until the
pairing is applied: each template sits on its own 5--7 scenes, and most of that
spread is scene difficulty rather than wording. No single template is resolvably
apart from its bucket once paired. The structure that does survive is at bucket
level above --- single-clause captions lose roughly four times what two-clause
ones lose, which is consistent with a shorter sentence leaving less to work
with. The \texttt{push} bucket is two scenes and carries no weight.
