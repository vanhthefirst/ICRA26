## The one-paragraph version

The 30 Aug handoff asked where the referent is lost. It is not lost: the sketch
encoder localises the circle to 2.8 px on held-out episodes, and the vector
`modality_attn` adds to the action tokens still localises it to 4.0 px. `pcla_v5`
simply never used it — deleting the sketch cost 0.4% of its action error, against
2.6% that was actually available. A new variant, `input_overlay`, was trained for
3000 steps to put the sketch upstream of the backbone. It is the first checkpoint
whose behaviour the sketch changes at all, and it still does not ground the
sketch: moving the circle onto the distractor costs the goal bowl 15.5 points,
but the circled object gains only 3.3 while an *unrelated* bowl gains 7.8. The
marker disturbs the model; it does not designate.

## What was measured, in order

**1. Linear readout of the sketch encoder** (`probe_sketch_readout.py`). Ridge
onto the circle centre, fit on 377 train episodes, scored on 41 val. Pixels at
the 256 render, where circle centres spread 67 px in x.

| read from | R^2 | median err |
|---|---|---|
| input mask itself (control) | 0.9985 | 1.2 px |
| mean of all 99 sketch tokens | 0.9785 | 2.1 px |
| circle-branch tokens | 0.9802 | 2.8 px |
| **vector added to every action token** | **0.9737** | **4.0 px** |

Off-distribution (2000 synthetic rings) the branch is lossier: 0.823 / 18.7 px
against the mean token's 0.911 / 12.5 px.

**2. The cross-attention is uniform** (`probe_sketch_attention.py`, 41 val
scenes). Entropy 6.6288 bits against 6.6294 for exactly uniform, on every scene
individually. Attention centroid pinned at x 0.5020 +- 0.0019 while true circle
centres span 0.4718 +- 0.2661. Moving the ring 40% of the frame moves the
centroid 0.02% of it. `tanh(attn_gate)` 0.0879, branch 9.0% of the residual
stream by rms.

Not for want of structure to attend to: `media_spread` 1.34, `key_spread` 1.24 —
the tokens and their keys differ from one another by more than their own mean
magnitude. The logits are just tiny (a random unit query sees a spread of 0.066).
The block is doing mean-pool-then-project, and mean pooling happens to preserve
position linearly, which is why row 4 of the table above survives.

**3. What the sketch does to the action chunk.** Ring 0.40 of the frame apart,
pinned noise, arm dims: rms 0.00202, of which 0.326 is not constant across the
horizon. The language control (two captions naming different objects): rms
0.1235, varying 0.426. So the pathway **can** reshape the chunk and is **55x too
weak** to matter (median over scenes; never below 12x). Ground-truth arm deltas
have std ~0.39, so the sketch moves the actions by ~0.5% of their scale.

**4. It is not used.** `validate.py --ablate-sketch` on the paired corpus's val
split, noise pinned: +0.000442 arm L1 at frame 0 (0.41%), +0.000133 over four
frames per episode. Gripper sign accuracy identical to four decimals.

**5. The corpus is not the excuse** (`probe_corpus_necessity.py`). At frame 0,
where the arm is at its home pose and the sketch is the only disambiguator, an
oracle told which task an episode came from gains:

| pair | blind L1 | oracle L1 | gain | d' |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t1\|t2 | 0.0802 | 0.0667 | +0.0134 | 1.18 |
| t3\|t4 | 0.0704 | 0.0457 | +0.0248 | 1.95 |
| t5\|t6 | 0.0749 | 0.0437 | +0.0312 | 2.91 |
| t7\|t8 | 0.0900 | 0.0543 | +0.0357 | 2.37 |
| mean | | | **+0.0263** | **2.10** |

`pcla_v5` captured 0.00044 of 0.0263 — **1.7%**. The signal was there.

**6. `overlay_v6_paired`**: `input_overlay` (sketch drawn into the frame the
frozen SigLIP reads, replacing `base_0_rgb`), same paired corpus, batch 32, same
LR schedule, 3000 steps, 2 h 23 m on one A100. Loss 0.0697 -> 0.0120. At matched
step 1400 it is 0.0170 against v5's 0.0167 — the variants fit the data equally
well, so any difference is in *what* was fitted.

Offline ablation, frame 0: **+0.03652** arm L1 (v5: +0.00044), with the
with-sketch error dropping 0.109 -> 0.081. Caveat: for this variant emptying the
sketch changes `base_0_rgb` itself, and the model never saw an unmarked frame, so
part of that is distribution shock rather than use.

**7. The rollout gate.** Spatial, ambiguous captions, 37 scenes x 14 rollouts per
arm, `--rotate180`. Percentages of grasps. v5's rows reproduce its published
table exactly under the same code.

| arm | success | grasped | bowl_1 (goal) | bowl_2 (distractor) | bowl_3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v5 pcla, real *(circle on bowl_1)* | 38.6% | 95.6% | 59.0% | 15.2% | 11.1% |
| v5 pcla, swap *(circle on bowl_2)* | 39.6% | 94.0% | 59.8% | 14.2% | 11.7% |
| v6 overlay, real | 40.3% | 89.6% | **78.7%** | 10.1% | 5.6% |
| v6 overlay, swap | 35.5% | 88.0% | **63.2%** | 13.4% | 13.4% |

## The dangerous part: the sketch's behaviour changed, and it looks like success

`pcla_v5` was easy to read — real, blank and swap all sat at 59%, so the sketch
did nothing and no number could be mistaken for grounding.

`overlay_v6` is not easy to read, and this is the thing to be careful about.

* Its **real arm alone looks like a breakthrough**: 59.0% -> 78.7% on taking the
  goal bowl, +19.7 points. Reported on its own it would read as "the sketch now
  works".
* The swap arm says otherwise. Moving the circle costs the goal bowl 15.5 points
  (78.7 -> 63.2, ~5 sigma), so the sketch is genuinely causal — v5's equivalent
  was +0.8, i.e. nothing. **But the freed mass does not go to the circled
  object.** bowl_2, the circled one, gains 3.3 points (10.1 -> 13.4, ~1.6 sigma,
  marginal). bowl_3, uncircled and irrelevant, gains 7.8 (5.6 -> 13.4, ~4 sigma).
* A pointer would drive the circled object up sharply. This drives an unrelated
  object up harder. The signature is a marker that **degrades the scene
  representation**, not one that designates a referent.
* Success does not follow the improvement either: 40.3% real vs 35.5% swap, and
  grasping is ~6 points below v5 in both arms. It picks the right bowl far more
  often and converts less of it.

This is the second time this project has had a number that moves for the wrong
reason. `sketch_l2` was the first — it rose to 0.029 while the model ignored the
sketch entirely. **Do not report the real arm without the swap arm.** The pair is
the measurement; either one alone is misleading.

## What drifted from the original plan

* **The 30 Aug handoff said "Do not run more training or more rollouts first —
  both can only re-confirm the null."** We ran both. The three probes it asked
  for came first and did change the diagnosis, so the order was respected; but
  the session still ended in a 3000-step train and a 2 x 518-rollout eval, which
  is what that line was written to prevent. Whether it was worth it is now
  answerable: it produced the first non-null sketch effect and the first
  ambiguous-looking result.
* **Two hypotheses were formed, published, and disproved within the same day.**
  Both are in STATE.md's retraction list; neither should be re-derived.
  1. *"The sketch is destroyed at the cross-attention."* No — a uniform softmax
     returns the mean of the values, and `to_kv` is unbiased, so it discards
     nothing.
  2. *"The channel can only translate the action chunk, not reshape it."* No —
     0.326 of the difference varies across the horizon, against language's 0.426.
     It reshapes; it is 55x too weak.
  A third, *"the corpus does not require the sketch"*, was measured and also
  refuted (item 5 above).
* **`sketch_l2` is now fully retired.** It was already known not to be a success
  criterion; for `input_overlay` it is worse than useless, because
  `sensitivity.measure_sensitivity` perturbs `observation.circle` and that
  variant never reads that field. The gates were disabled for `overlay_v6` so a
  healthy run would not print ~0 and be stopped.
* **The diagnostic budget held.** 3000 steps was the cap and 3000 steps is what
  ran.

## Two things we have NOT done, and must

### A. The two repos disagree, and nothing records which version produced a result

This is unresolved and it undermines every comparison in this file. Measured on
the pod at the end of the session:

* The pod's training repo sits at `265fe94` while origin is at `7d3527c`. It is
  behind because **pods cannot `git fetch` non-interactively** — the remote is
  https and prompts for credentials — so code reaches them as base64 over the
  tty. The working tree therefore drifts from any commit: at session end it had
  four modified tracked files and four untracked ones, all shipped by hand.
* `git stash list` on the pod shows **three** stashes, from three different
  sessions: this session's parked eval edits, a `WIP on 65101ba`, and a
  `pre-gate-validation 2026-08-28`. Old divergent code is sitting there waiting
  to be restored by accident.
* **`/workspace/harness_repo` is not a git repository at all** — pods receive a
  bundle of `outputs/`, so the harness that produced a rollout has no version
  identity whatsoever.

That is the mechanism by which "the code produces two different results". It is
not mysterious; it is that neither repo's version is pinned at the point a number
is generated.

Minimum fix, in order:
1. Write the git SHA of both repos into every results file and every probe json
   at the moment it is produced. Nothing else can be trusted until this exists.
2. Give the harness bundle a `VERSION` file carrying the eval repo's SHA, written
   by whatever builds the bundle.
3. Make pods able to fetch — an ssh remote with a deploy key, or a read-only
   token in the pod env — so shipping by tty stops being the normal path.
4. Inspect and then clear the three stale stashes.

### B. "Moving the target object degrades almost every VLA" — never tested

This is the hypothesis that a VLA's performance collapses when an object sits
somewhere it did not sit in training, independently of anything to do with
sketches. It has never been measured here, and it matters more than it looks:

* The paired corpus is *built* by relocating a bowl into its partner layout's
  position. If relocation alone costs a large amount of performance, then the
  ~40% ceiling every arm in this project runs into may be a relocation artifact
  rather than a grounding failure — and the sketch results would be being read
  against a floor nobody has established.
* It also bounds what any sketch model can achieve on this corpus, which is
  exactly the number needed to say whether 78.7% is good.

Proposed test, and it needs no sketch model:
take stock `pi05_libero` — already measured at **96.7%** on upright spatial —
and evaluate it with explicit captions on (a) the shipped scenes and (b) the same
scenes with the target bowl moved to the partner layout's position. The gap
between (a) and (b) is the relocation cost, cleanly, with no sketch pathway in
the loop. `scripts/replay_donor_pose.py` already does the relocation, and
`outputs/paired_spatial_bddl/replay_pair_matrix.log` has the replay success rates
that bound how many scenes are usable.

Until A and B are done, treat every comparison in this document as provisional.

## Traps found this session

* **Orbax has no subtree restore.** `PyTreeRestore` rejects an item whose tree
  does not match the on-disk one. Read all 5.8 GB and index; it comes off the
  volume at ~730 MiB/s, so this is 9 seconds, not minutes.
* **`scp` does not work through the RunPod proxy** ("Connection closed"), and
  `ssh` without `-tt` is refused ("Your SSH client doesn't support PTY").
* **The pty eats the first line of output.** A base64 stream sent to stdout
  arrives without its gzip header. Buffer to a file on the pod, prefix each line
  with a marker, and pad with two throwaway lines.
* **`logging.basicConfig` is a no-op once jax is imported** — absl claims the
  root logger first. Both probes logged nothing on their first run, including a
  self-check number. Pass `force=True`.
* **A restarted container loses its ephemeral disk**, and two things live there
  that the eval needs. `~/.libero/config.yaml`, without which every shard dies on
  LIBERO's first-import prompt with `EOFError`; and `libEGL.so.1`, without which
  every shard dies inside `import mujoco` with `'NoneType' object has no
  attribute 'eglQueryString'`. Both now preflighted in `run_eval.sh` (`7d3527c`),
  which also pins `__EGL_VENDOR_LIBRARY_FILENAMES` to the NVIDIA ICD so libglvnd
  cannot hand MuJoCo the mesa one and render on the CPU.
* **`fine_tune.keep_period` was never wired into `build_train_config`** — it has
  silently done nothing on every run to date, so each run kept only its newest
  checkpoint. Fixed in `8dc25c0`; it matters at 30000 steps.
* **An A100 in CA-MTL-3 is not always rentable.** One was released mid-session
  and the replacement took a while. Check whether a measurement needs the GPU at
  all before giving one up — once the attention was known to be uniform, the
  entire step-3 result needed 34M parameters and would have run on a CPU.
